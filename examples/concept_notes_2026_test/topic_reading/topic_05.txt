CONCEPT NOTE First Session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance 6<U+2013>7 July 2026 I Palexpo, Geneva <U+201C>Sustainable AI, Nature and the Governance Gap:<U+201D> Format: High-Level Moderated Panel Discussion followed by an interactive discussion Venue: Room K Duration: 45 minutes Time: 7:30 to 8:15 AM Side-Event Co-organizers:  Campaign for Nature, French Government, UNEP, ITU, UNESCO,  Sustainable AI Coalition Description: This high-level side event will bring together governments, international organizations, Indigenous  Peoples and local communities leaders, industry representatives, academia and civil society to  discuss the most pressing priorities for advancing environmentally sustainable AI governance. The discussion will explore the many facets of the nexus between AI and nature, including the  environmental footprint of AI, the use of AI to benefit the planet, and one fundamental yet often  overlooked  dimension  of  this  relationship:  the  downstream  impacts  of  AI-driven  economic  acceleration on biodiversity and the natural systems that sustain human civilization. The conversation will also highlight how governance of AI to ensure its environmental sustainability  ranges from international frameworks to the internal policies of AI companies, and how it must be  grounded in dialogue among Indigenous Peoples and local communities leaders, policymakers,  scientists, civil society, and AI industry representatives on the relationship between AI systems and  natural systems. Rather than positioning AI and nature as opposing forces, the session will explore what governance  principles, safeguards, accountability mechanisms, and value systems may be needed at the    international  level  down  to  the  companies<U+2019>  level  to  help  ensure  that  AI  contributes  to  an   environmentally responsible future rather than accelerating environmental decline. Objectives of the session: As existing nature coalitions and dedicated AI-centered coalitions, along with multi-stakeholder  initiatives  and  other  actors,  are  starting to  advocate  for  the  advancement  of  dialogue  and  collaboration at the intersection of AI governance and nature, the session will catalyze this  momentum. The session will crowdsource initial principles and governance priorities for 'Environmentally  Responsible AI' through interactive audience participation during and after the event. 25 minutes Panel discussion <U+F0B7> Moderators (panel and open discussion): Sally Radwan,  Chief Digital Officer, UNEP & Tomas Lamanauskas,  Deputy Secretary General, ITU <U+F0B7> Hindou Oumarou Ibrahim, Indigenous leader, member  and former chair of UN Permanent Forum on Indigenous  Issues <U+F0B7> Mariya Gabriel, Assistant Director-General  Communication and Information, UNESCO <U+F0B7> H.E. The session brings together policymakers, industry leaders,  and civil society to explore workforce transition strategies, reskilling frameworks, and inclusive  governance design. It calls for co-developed frameworks that ensure AI-driven growth benefits all  workers, not just capital holders. Background and focus Artificial intelligence is no longer a distant technological prospect, it is actively reshaping labour  markets across the world, transforming the nature of work at a pace that public policy, education  systems, and social protection frameworks have yet to match. While AI is displacing routine and  repetitive roles across sectors, it is simultaneously generating entirely new categories of work that  demand new competencies, new institutional arrangements, and new governance approaches.  The central challenge of our time is not whether AI will change work, it will and it already has, but  whether this transformation will be managed in a way that is fair, inclusive, and equitable. History  has shown that technological transitions, when left ungoverned, tend to concentrate gains among  capital holders while distributing costs among workers. The AI transition risks following the same  pattern unless deliberate action is taken by governments, industry, and civil society working  together. The Global Dialogue on AI Governance, convened by the United Nations, represents a historic  opportunity to establish the foundational frameworks that will govern AI's role in economies and  societies. The session on AI and Job Creation sits at the heart of this agenda, addressing the human  dimension of AI deployment; the workers, communities, and institutions most affected by this  transition. Organized by the Internet Governance Forum's Dynamic Coalition on Digital Economy, Project  CREATE (Collaborate to Realize Employment & Entrepreneurship For All Through Technology  Ecosystem), and Health Parliament, this session brings together leading voices from policy,  industry, and civil society to examine workforce transition strategies, reskilling frameworks, and  inclusive policy design. The session urges co-development of governance frameworks that ensure  AI-driven growth benefits all workers, not just capital holders, and advances the vision of Internet  for All and Livelihood for All.

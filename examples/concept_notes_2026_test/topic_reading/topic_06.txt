CONCEPT NOTE First Session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance 6<U+2013>7 July 2026 I Palexpo, Geneva From Gatekeepers to Global Interoperability: Accountability,  Market Power and Translator Layers in AI Governance Date & Time    :   Tuesday 7 July 2026, 12:00<U+2013>12:45 CEST, Location: Room F Organizers: 1) Alyne Andrade Bezerra  is a lawyer, lecturer, and PhD in Law from the  University of Lisbon, specializing in Competition Law, Digital Regulation, Artificial  Intelligence, and Intellectual Property. She serves as Director of International  Relations at the Brazilian Institute of Information Technology Law (IBDI), with  research  focused  on  digital  markets,  platform  governance,  and  emerging  technologies in the European Union and Latin America. 2) Nidhi Singh is an Advocate-on-Record at the Supreme Court of India and Panel  Counsel (Service Laws) at the Delhi High Court, with litigation practice extending  to US District and State Superior Courts in California, New York, Texas, and  Arizona. She holds a JSM from Stanford Law School, an MSc from Oxford Law as  Louis-Dreyfus Weidenfeld Scholar, Public Policy from Harvard, and a PG. Diploma  in Economics for Competition Law from King's College London. A Non-Resident  Fellow at the Dhirubhai Ambani School of Law, AI Policy Lead at RegHorizon,  Switzerland, and visiting faculty at leading Indian National Law Universities, her  research  spans  Constitutional  litigation,  Competition  law,  algorithmic  accountability,  arbitration  and  interoperability  mechanisms  for  global  AI  governance. Background and focus Artificial intelligence is being governed through a rapidly multiplying patchwork  of instruments: the EU AI Act and Digital Markets Act, Brazil<U+2019>s draft AI framework  (PL 2338/2023), India<U+2019>s emerging judicial and Ministerial sectoral guidance, the   Council of Europe Framework Convention, and a growing body of  soft-law  standards. This fragmentation creates two intertwined risks. First, compliance  burdens  concentrate  advantage  in  the  largest  firms  -  the  same  digital  gatekeepers whose market power AI now amplifies through control of compute,  foundation models, data, and distribution channels. Second, jurisdictions in the  Global South risk becoming rule-takers, importing frameworks calibrated to  other markets without the institutional capacity or interoperability mechanisms  needed to adapt them. This session merges two complementary proposals -  IBDI<U+2019>s <U+201C>AI Governance and  Digital Gatekeepers: Accountability, Interoperability and Market Power in the  European Union<U+201D> (ID 79) and <U+201C>From Fragmentation to Interoperability - Translator  Layers for Global AI Governance<U+201D> (ID 85) - into a single conversation across the  European Union, Latin America, and South Asia. It examines (i) how EU-style  gatekeeper regulation addresses accountability and market power in AI value  chains, and what lessons and limits it holds for other regions; and (ii) whether  <U+201C>translator layers<U+201D> - technical, legal, and institutional mechanisms that map  obligations across regimes - can move global AI governance from fragmentation  to interoperability without forcing harmonization. Drawing on Brazilian, Indian, and European perspectives, speakers will explore  concrete design choices: mutual recognition of conformity assessments, model  documentation standards that travel across jurisdictions, interoperable audit  and incident-reporting formats, and the role of courts and regulators in the  Global South as active shapers rather than passive recipients of AI governance  norms. The objective is a set of actionable propositions for the Global Dialogue  on  how  accountability  for  powerful  AI  actors  and  interoperability  across  governance regimes can be pursued together rather than traded off against each  other. Speakers and Format The session will run for 45 minutes as a moderated roundtable designed for  interaction. Confirmed speakers: Dra. Alyne Andrade de Oliveira Bezerra (Director  of International Relations, IBDI) on EU gatekeeper regulation, digital markets,  and platform governance in the EU and Latin America; Ms. Nidhi Singh (Advocate- on-Record, Supreme Court of India) on translator layers, interoperability, and  algorithmic accountability from a Global South litigation and policy perspective;  and  Mr. Anurag Vijay (Advocate, Jharkhand High Court, India) on comparative  regulatory design and implementation in India.  Run of show: opening framing by the co-organizers (7 minutes); a moderated  exchange  structured  around  three  questions  -  who  is  accountable  for  AI  gatekeeper power, what would interoperability actually require, and what role   should the Global South play (23 minutes); open discussion with the audience (12  minutes); and closing takeaways for the Global Dialogue (3 minutes). The format  prioritizes dialogue over presentations: no slides, short interventions, and more  than a quarter of the session reserved for audience participation, reflecting the  co-equal collaboration between the organizing teams. CONCEPT NOTE First Session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance 6<U+2013>7 July 2026 I Palexpo, Geneva Comparing National AI-in-Education Policy Approaches: Towards  Building a Shared Framework Date & Time:  7 July 2026, 12:30<U+2013>13:30 CEST  Organizers: UNESCO Link: Online, via Zoom Background and focus Artificial intelligence (AI) has an increasing presence in all facets of education,  challenging the fundamental assumptions about the role of education to develop the  knowledge, skills and confidence needed to participate fully in society. In view of the  rapid shifts associated with this technology, many governments have shown interest in  developing coherent national policies for AI in education, but may lack institutional  capacity, technical expertise, and contextual evidence to create a workable strategy.  In this context, this session will look to build on the shared foundation set by UNESCO<U+2019>s  Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research and its twin AI Competency Framework  for Teachers and AI Competency Framework for Students while seeking to deepen  understanding of the ways in which policy and practice around AI in education converge  and differ across diverse contexts. Offering a rich dialogue between academic experts  and experienced policy-makers, it will surface and compare approaches of education  policy-makers against a common set of dimensions, identifying shared building blocks,  context-specific divergences, and unresolved gaps, with the aim of producing a  comparative mapping that can inform the development of a comprehensive AI in   Education policy framework for Ministries of Education and a complementary tool to  assess <U+2018>readiness<U+2019>, building upon existing UNESCO guidance. CONCEPT NOTE    First Session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance  6<U+2013>7 July 2026 I Palexpo, Geneva    Building Trust and Governance in the Era of Generative AI: Intellectual Property,  Quality, Reliability and Accountability      Date & Time: Tuesday 7 July 2026, 12:45<U+2013>13:30 CEST (45 minutes)    Organizers: Co-organised, co-equal collaboration: Russian Academy of National Economy and  Public Administration (RANEPA) and Ivannikov Institute for System Programming of the Russian  Academy of Sciences (ISP RAS), Russian Federation; with S.M for Intellectual Property (Egypt), an  intellectual-property and innovation advisory firm, and iPioneer, an AI<U+2013>intellectual-property  governance and legal-technology platform (MENA region and the Global South).    Background and focus  As generative and multimodal AI systems move into public services, media, education, the creative  industries and the wider economy, two governance questions are converging. The first is whether  we can trust what these systems produce <U+2014> how reliability, quality and robustness are measured  rather than merely asserted, and how such assessments can travel across borders. The second is  who is accountable for, and who holds rights over, AI outputs <U+2014> how provenance, attribution and  intellectual-property frameworks keep pace with content that machines now generate at scale.  Both questions expose the same underlying gap: a shortage of shared, interoperable and  independently verifiable approaches. And both fall hardest on developing countries and emerging  regions that must assess and govern systems they did not build.  This session brings together two complementary communities of practice. One works on the  evaluation, verification and trust assessment of large language and multimodal models, combining  public-administration and systems-engineering expertise. The other works on intellectual property  and the broader legal, regulatory and governance dimension of AI <U+2014> including AI governance, legal  capacity-building and the innovation ecosystems of startups and SMEs <U+2014> with particular attention  to the MENA region and other developing economies. By placing reliability evaluation and  IP/provenance governance in the same conversation, the session moves from the abstract  principle of <U+201C>trustworthy AI<U+201D> toward concrete, comparable practices for measuring quality and for  establishing accountability and rights over AI-generated content.  The focus is deliberately practical and cross-regional. Speakers will compare how trust and rights  are handled in different jurisdictions, identify where frameworks diverge and where they can be   made interoperable without imposing a single rigid standard, and surface capacity-building needs  and partnership opportunities. The aim is to produce actionable input <U+2014> captured in a short key- messages summary for the Joint Secretariat <U+2014> that supports the constructive, equitable and  accountable development of AI worldwide.    Speakers and Format  Format. A 45-minute moderated panel designed for interaction and cross-regional exchange. A  single moderator <U+2014> provided by the RANEPA side or, if preferred, a mutually agreed neutral  moderator <U+2014> steers one integrated conversation so that technical-evaluation and  legal/intellectual-property perspectives respond directly to one another rather than running in  parallel. After a short framing, each panellist gives a brief opening position; the moderator then  threads shared questions across both themes, followed by an open audience Q&A and a closing  round of key messages. Delivered in English; no interpretation required.  Moderator. Dr. Pavel Golosov, Director of the Institute for Social Sciences, RANEPA.  Speakers. The panel brings together complementary expertise for a balanced, multidisciplinary  discussion. From the RANEPA side, the proposed list of speakers cover:   -  Ms. Oksana Sotnikova, Director of the Department of Strategic Development and  Innovation, Ministry of Economic Development of the Russian Federation <U+2014> AI policy and  the governance of AI research and intellectual property;   -  Dr. Arutyun Avetisian, ISP RAS Evaluation, <U+2014> verification & trust assessment of AI systems;  -  Mr. Dmitry Ontoev, Department of Information Technology of the City of Moscow <U+2014> AI in  smart cities and the digitalisation of urban governance;   -  Mr. Alexey Shpilman, Director for AI Technology Development, T-Technologies <U+2014> AI in  cybersecurity.   From the intellectual-property side:  -  Ms. Shrouk Abdelaziz, Founder & CEO of S.M for Intellectual Property and Founder of  iPioneer <U+2014> AI governance and intellectual property from a regional and practical perspective  (MENA and the Global South);  -  Further speakers <U+2014> covering international intellectual-property law and governance and,  where feasible, AI governance and public policy from an international perspective <U+2014> are  being finalised.

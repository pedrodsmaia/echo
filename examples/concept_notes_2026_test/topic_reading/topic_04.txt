The session, delivered  in Spanish, will offer a demonstrative introduction to the Responsible AI Organizational Maturity  Self-Assessment Framework, the tool that helps companies identify their starting point and  implement a roadmap toward responsible AI.  Background and focus  Artificial intelligence is no longer optional for Latin American MSMEs and startups: it is a condition  for competing. However, many organizations in the region still lack the frameworks, tools, and  support needed to adopt it responsibly and competitively. This session responds directly to that  gap.  Adigital and BID Lab, through fAIr LAC, present IAm<U+00E9>ricas, their joint initiative to drive the  responsible adoption of AI among MSMEs, startups, and innovation ecosystem stakeholders  across Latin America and the Caribbean. The project has already built a regional community of  more than 3000 participants and a network of implementation partners in Chile, Uruguay, Ecuador,  Mexico, Colombia, and soon Brazil, working toward concrete targets such as 1500 startups and  MSMEs adopting Responsible AI.  Participating in this event is relevant because it introduces attendees to one of IAm<U+00E9>ricas' core  tools: the Responsible AI Organizational Maturity Self-Assessment Framework, which helps  companies identify where they stand, based on criteria such as strategy, governance, risk  management, and training, among others, and implement a roadmap to strengthen their AI  governance.   The session, which will be delivered in Spanish, will open with a presentation of IAm<U+00E9>ricas and its  three components: awareness, training, and tools, followed by an awareness talk and an  interactive activity introducing the Self-Assessment Framework. Given the session's format, this  activity will be demonstrative: giving participants a clear sense of how the tool works and what kind  of insights they can gain from completing it.  The expected outcome is that each participant leaves with a clear understanding of what the self- assessment is, why it matters for their organization, and the motivation to join the formal process  to obtain their own diagnosis and roadmap. CONCEPT NOTE First Session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance 6<U+2013>7 July 2026 I Palexpo, Geneva A Common Language for AI Governance: An Open Vocabulary and  Toolkit for Multistakeholder Adoption Across Spheres of Influence Date & Time:  Tuesday, 7 July 2026, 12:00<U+2013>12:45 CEST (online side event) Organizers:  AI Integrity Organization (AIO), Geneva, Switzerland Background and focus AI governance increasingly depends on a shared understanding of what AI systems  actually prioritise, yet policymakers, regulators, domain professionals and civil-society  actors still describe AI behaviour in different and often incompatible vocabularies <U+2014>  <U+201C>bias,<U+201D> <U+201C>fairness,<U+201D> <U+201C>alignment,<U+201D> <U+201C>trustworthiness.<U+201D> Without a common, measurable  reference point, multistakeholder cooperation remains principled in intent but  fragmented in practice. This session introduces an open, MIT-licensed vocabulary and toolkit that any  stakeholder can adopt without specialised technical training. It is grounded in Schwartz  basic-values theory <U+2014> validated across more than 80 cultures <U+2014> and operationalised  through 366,120 forced-choice measurements across eight commercial AI models in  seven professional domains (S. Lee, 2026b; arXiv:2604.11216). Three findings frame the  policy stakes: the models reverse their value priorities in 37<U+2013>50% of cases when the same  dilemma is reframed from another stakeholder<U+2019>s perspective; all eight restructure their  value hierarchies in defence contexts, with Security surging to dominance; and in  education several prioritise institutional compliance over learner self-direction at rates  above 85%. AI systems, in short, do not hold stable value commitments <U+2014> a foundational   challenge for any governance framework that assumes transparent, predictable  reasoning. The evidence is paired with the AIO 20002 logging standard, an open-source instrument  that maps AI reasoning to structured value/evidence/source codes designed to support  the reasoning-traceability goals of EU AI Act Articles 12<U+2013>14 (a voluntary instrument, not a  certified compliance mechanism) and deployable today, ahead of the Act<U+2019>s 2 August 2026  milestones. The session speaks directly to the cluster on Safe, secure and trustworthy AI:  interoperability and compatibility of approaches, while lowering the technical-fluency  barrier that keeps non-technical voices out of AI-governance debate. By giving every  participant a concrete, shared reference for what a model prioritised and why, it turns  multistakeholder collaboration from a stated principle into a working method each actor  can apply within their own sphere of influence. Speakers and Format Format (45 minutes, online).  The session pairs a short evidence briefing with two interactive instruments used for  outcome measurement rather than decoration. Opening (3 min) frames the core  message and a sphere-of-influence matrix.  Part 1 <U+2014> The Shared Vocabulary (12 min), delivered by Seulki Lee, Founder of the AI  Integrity Organization (Geneva), presents the empirical findings and the vocabulary chain  from Schwartz values to the Authority Stack model to the AIO 20002 logging standard. Part 2 <U+2014> Bring It Home (10 min) runs a live clinical-scenario poll, compares audience  judgement with measured AI behaviour, and demonstrates AIO 20002 logging. Part 3 <U+2014>  Govern AI From Your Seat (15 min) is a moderated roundtable of invited international  experts spanning regulatory compliance (Europe), AI security and governance (Africa),  and applied AI evaluation (Asia-Pacific), each showing how the vocabulary applies in their  sphere of influence. A closing segment (5 min) captures adoption-intent polling and  follow-up. Participants leave with three free, ready-to-use assets <U+2014> a Vocabulary Reference Card, a  one-page <U+201C>Get Started in Your Sphere<U+201D> guide, and an open Call for Co-authors to the AIO  Working Paper Series <U+2014> and AIO will share a multistakeholder synthesis report with the  Joint Secretariat after the event.  Focal Point/Contact:  Seulki Lee, Founder & CEO, AI Integrity Organization (AIO) <U+2014> 2sk@aioq.org <U+2014> aioq.org

CONCEPT NOTE First Session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance 6<U+2013>7 July 2026 I Palexpo, Geneva Workshop on <U+201C>Equity Lens: AI Guardrails for Fair Talent Selection,  Recognition, and Opportunity<U+201D> Date & Time:  Monday, 6 July 2026 | 14:00<U+2013>14:45 CEST Organizers:  Working Group on Equal Opportunities (AKC) of the German Physical Society (DPG) Co-organizer: Sumiko Shimo, Expert member of UNESCO AI Ethics without Borders &  Women for Ethical AI, G20 Technical Facility, ISO/IEC SC42 on AI Contributors:  Dr. Iris Traulsen, AIP Leibniz Institute for Astrophysics Potsdam Sebastian Dohrmann, German Physical Society Background and focus As AI becomes part of institutional decision-making, including decisions about selection,  recognition, funding, leadership opportunities, and career advancement, governance  frameworks must ensure that these systems do not reproduce existing inequities. Many  selection processes are already shaped by unconscious bias and structural patterns that  are difficult to detect, such as reliance on familiar names, concentration within certain  institutions or networks, repeat visibility, and unequal assessment across career stages.  Equity Lens responds to this challenge by exploring how AI-supported transparency  mechanisms can help make such patterns visible before decisions are finalized. This workshop invites participants to co-design the next stage of Equity Lens, a prototype  transparency tool designed to support fairer, more evidence-based selection processes.  Rather than automating decisions or replacing human judgment, Equity Lens provides  privacy-preserving, aggregated indicators of potential structural bias before committees   reach final decisions. The tool does not rank candidates, recommend outcomes, collect  individual votes, or influence committee choices. Its purpose is to help decision-makers  recognize patterns that might otherwise remain invisible, support informed reflection, and  keep responsibility firmly with human decision-makers. This session is for participants who want to help turn AI governance principles into  practical tools for fairer decision-making. Participants will test the Equity Lens concept,  share concerns and use cases from their own sectors, and help define the requirements,  safeguards, and design priorities for an open-source version of the tool. By joining,  participants can contribute directly to a practical requirements specification and help  shape an approach that organizations with different levels of technical capacity could  adapt for responsible, rights-respecting selection processes. Speakers and Format Format: Interactive co-creation workshop This workshop brings together perspectives from scientific practice, equality initiatives, AI  ethics,  standardization,  and  responsible  technology  governance.  It  moves  beyond  knowledge exchange toward developing practical requirements for ethical transparency  tools. Provisional speakers: Ruzin Aganoglu, PhD <U+2013> Physicist, CTO of Lab-on-Fiber GmbH, and Deputy Spokesperson of  the Working Group on Equal Opportunities (AKC) of the German Physical Society (DPG). She  advances equity initiatives in physics, promotes STEM role models, and builds national and  international networks. She will provide a science<U+2013>industry innovation perspective and  introduce practical challenges from research communities and professional societies that  motivated Equity Lens. Sumiko Shimo  <U+2013> Independent researcher and expert member of UNESCO AI Ethics  Without  Borders,  Women4Ethical  AI,  the  G20  Technical  Facility,  ISO  SC42  on  AI  standardization, and CAIDP. She specializes in responsible AI, ethical innovation, bias  mitigation, and inclusive technology governance. She will facilitate ethical reflection,  connecting  Equity  Lens  with  principles  of  fairness,  transparency,  privacy  protection,  accountability, and human oversight. Agenda: 5 min <U+2013> Introduction and context 10 min <U+2013> Prototype demonstration and roadmap  20 min <U+2013> Discussion and feedback 10 min <U+2013> Next steps The workshop will deliver a stakeholder-informed framework defining ethical principles,  governance safeguards, and technical requirements for transparent selection processes.

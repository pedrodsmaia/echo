AI Accountability in Practice: Financial  Standards, Compliance and Operational  Guardrails Organizers 1. Pride AI and Business Solutions (prideaiandbusinesssolutions.com) 2. DocsOrb (docsorb.com) Concept Note Part A: Background and Focus AI is becoming core economic and operational infrastructure, but the knowledge gap on AI  Governance is not up to speed. Financial institutions and regulators still lack reliable methods to  assess the real value, defensibility, and downside risk of AI systems. At the same time,  organizations adopting AI tools often rely on static policies, manual reviews, and after-the-fact  audits, leaving data exposure, shadow AI use, and compliance violations unmanaged in real  time. This side-event presents AI accountability through two connected pillars. Financial Accountability via <U+201C>IFRS for AI<U+201D>: Pride AI introduces a framework for recognizing  and measuring AI systems as auditable computational assets. It addresses the <U+201C>capital erasure<U+201D>  problem in current accounting models, where continuous investment in data, GPU or  engineering is valued through four key pillars: Recognition, Measurement, Disclosure, and  Governance. Through asset manifests, data provenance, defensibility measures, and valuation  models, IFRS for AI aims to help investors, central banks, and sovereign funds distinguish  genuine technical moats from fragile AI wrappers. Operational Enforcement via Policy-to-Guardrail Governance: DocsOrb focuses on how  organizations turn written policies into enforceable AI controls. In most organizations,  compliance, legal, HR, risk, security, and data protection teams define what AI use is allowed,  restricted, or blocked, but enforcement remains manual or technical. DocsOrb bridges that gap  by translating organizational policies into AI guardrails, detecting risky use, preventing sensitive  data exposure, and generating audit evidence. Together, these pillars move AI governance beyond principles. The session explores how AI  can be valued, governed, enforced, and audited through practical frameworks that support trust,  compliance, cybersecurity, and institutional accountability.  Part B: Speakers <U+25CF> Tamiru Belayneh Hassen (Founder & CEO, Pride AI and Business Solutions;  Google Cloud Partner): Tamiru works at the intersection of finance, technology,  transformation, and governance. As Founder and CEO of Pride AI and Business  Solutions, he brings more than 20 years of experience across financial services,  institutional banking, operations, leadership, and regulatory compliance in Ethiopia and  the United States. His work focuses on AI strategy, governance, security, and financial  governance solutions, including the emerging <U+201C>IFRS for AI<U+201D> framework for valuing and  reporting AI systems as accountable computational assets. <U+25CF> Santhosh Komaragiri (Founder & CEO, DocsOrb): Santhosh brings over 8 years of  experience in data privacy, compliance, and cybersecurity, serving mid-market and  enterprise organizations. He surfaces a simple principle: engineers implement controls,  but organizations own the policy. Through DocsOrb, compliance, legal, HR, risk, and  security teams can define what AI use is allowed, restricted, or blocked, while technical  teams implement those decisions with audit evidence. CONCEPT NOTE First Session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance 6<U+2013>7 July 2026 I Palexpo, Geneva Information Integrity in the Age of AI: Building Trust, Countering  Disinformation, and Protecting People Online Date & Time:  Tuesday, 7 July 2026, 07:30<U+2013>08:15 (45 minutes) Organizers:  Fondation Abeona / Global Trust Challenge, and the European  Broadcasting Union (EBU) Background and focus This session brings together the Global Trust Challenge<U+2014>a G7-mandated multi- stakeholder coalition building technical and policy tools in information integrity<U+2014>and the  European Broadcasting Union (EBU) public service media network to address the  systemic erosion of trust across the global information ecosystem. Generative AI is  accelerating both the scale and the sophistication of disinformation while simultaneously  entering the information ecosystem as a direct source audiences consult. Public trust in  news is under sustained pressure: the Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026  (University of Oxford, ~100,000 respondents across 48 markets) found overall news trust  at its lowest point since 2015 (37%), even as AI chatbot use for news reached 10% of  respondents and social media overtook news websites and apps as a news source (54%  vs. 51%). These trends converge with growing concern about AI-enabled harms that fall unevenly  on specific groups, including gendered disinformation and abuse targeting women  (notably women journalists and public figures) and risks to children from AI-generated  content and AI-driven platform design. The UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance  identifies information integrity, transparency, accountability and human rights protection  as core thematic pillars.  The Global Trust Challenge has emerged from the G7 Hiroshima process as a multi-  stakeholder coalition<U+2014>convened by founding partners including the OECD, IEEE, and  UNESCO, and spearheaded by AI Commons, Fondation Abeona, and others<U+2014>mandated  to develop information and policy tools to advance information integrity. The Global  Trust Challenge and the EBU each bring complementary, practitioner-level experience to  these questions: one from a multilateral coalition building technical and policy tools for  trust, the other from decades of editorial accountability and audience-trust research  across 68 public service media members in over 50 countries. This session is intended to connect that practitioner experience directly to the Dialogue's  deliberations on safe, secure and trustworthy AI and on human rights protection, ahead  of the Co-Chairs' summary. CONCEPT NOTE    First Session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance  6<U+2013>7 July 2026 I Palexpo, Geneva    Artificial Insecurity: AI and Cybersecurity       Date & Time:  7 July 2026, 12:00<U+2013>12:45 CEST     Organizers:  Access Now and Global Partners Digital (GPD)     Background and focus  300 words  This session brings together top experts from civil society, industry and government to explore how  cybersecurity and AI governance intersect. Looking beyond the hype, we propose a technically  grounded conversation on the cybersecurity implications of large language models and agentic AI.  Beyond just identifying risks, speakers will share concrete examples, challenge assumptions, and  discuss rights-respecting recommendations that strengthen security and public trust in advanced AI.     In particular, our discussion will focus on two topics: the impact of cutting edge AI tools on privacy and  encryption, as well as the threats and opportunities related to the use of AI tools for vulnerability  discovery in cybersecurity.    Regarding the impact of AI tools on privacy and encryption, questions to be asked include:  -<U+200B> What is the current state of the art regarding privacy and AI tools such as ChatGPT, Claude etc?  What interests or rights are being prioritized and what risks are created for at-risk communities?  -<U+200B> How do developments in agentic AI threaten privacy, and even encrypted communications, and  what can be done to mitigate these risks?  -<U+200B> How does the integration of LLMs across digital services, such as search engines, platforms,  and messaging tools, create threats to the digital security of at-risk users and how could things  be done differently?    On the use of AI tools for vulnerability discovering, questions include:  -<U+200B> What lessons can we learn from the recent furore around cutting edge AI models being used to  discover security vulnerabilities? What can we put down to hype and what do we need to take  seriously?  -<U+200B> Access to these advanced models has been a key issue, with only a limited number of  stakeholders being given access to Anthropic<U+2019>s Mythos model. How can we avoid a situation in  which robust security becomes dependent on privileged access to cutting edge AI models?

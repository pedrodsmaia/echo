CONCEPT NOTE First Session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance 6<U+2013>7 July 2026 I Palexpo, Geneva Intelligence Beyond Borders:  Empowering the Next Billion Toward an AI-Ready, Ethical and Equitable  Future Date & Time:  07 July 2026, 17:30<U+2013>18:30 CEST  Organizers:  AI and Quantum International Hub (AIQUAINT)  Background and focus Artificial intelligence is rapidly reshaping labor markets, education systems, and  governance structures worldwide <U+2014> yet the benefits and risks of this transformation are  not evenly distributed. For the "next billion" digital citizens, largely concentrated across  the Global South, AI presents both a historic opportunity for economic leapfrogging and  a serious risk of deepening existing inequalities. Millions of young people possess the  talent and ambition to shape the AI era, but structural gaps <U+2014> in infrastructure, digital  literacy, funding, and representation in policy conversations <U+2014> threaten to leave them as  consumers rather than co-creators of AI systems. This session moves beyond diagnosing the problem to showcasing what is already  working on the ground. Drawing on experience across Asia and the Middle East, the  panel will highlight national youth AI summits, "AI for Good" initiatives, and community- driven education programs that embed ethics and equity into AI literacy from the outset  <U+2014> rather than treating them as an afterthought. The UAE, as a hub connecting South  Asia, Africa, and the wider Global South, offers a unique vantage point for this discussion,  with speakers bringing direct, cross-regional experience in policy, legal frameworks, and  education.  Central to the conversation is the question of governance: how can young people move  from being subjects of AI policy to genuine participants in shaping it? The panel will  examine mechanisms for embedding youth voice into global AI governance frameworks,  including multilateral dialogues like the UN's own,  o that regulatory and ethical  standards reflect the realities of those who will live longest with AI's consequences. The  session aims to leave participants with concrete models for replication and a clearer  sense of the governance pathways available for youth-led advocacy. Speakers and Format The panel brings together three speakers with complementary expertise and shared  grounding in UAE-based, globally-connected work. Gowri Shankar, Founder of the AI and  Quantum International Hub and an AI Ethicist, brings a technologist's and ethicist's lens  on responsible AI design for emerging economies. Shakilla Jhungeer, Chief Legal and  Compliance Officer at the Global Alliance for Artificial Intelligence (GAFAI), contributes  expertise in international legal and regulatory frameworks governing AI deployment and  accountability. Christopher Abraham, CEO of SP Jain School of Global Management,  Dubai, offers an academic and institutional perspective on preparing the next generation  of leaders and professionals for an AI-driven economy. The 60-minute session will run as a moderated panel discussion, opening with brief  framing remarks from each speaker (3<U+2013>5 minutes) on their area of focus, followed by a  structured moderated dialogue drawing out cross-cutting themes <U+2014> access, ethics,  education, and governance. The final 15 minutes will be reserved for audience Q&A,  encouraging engagement from young participants and Global Dialogue delegates. The  format is designed to balance expert insight with accessibility for a broad, youth-inclusive  audience. Focal Point/Contact:  Gowri Shankar Sivabala (gowrishankergs@gmail.com)

CONCEPT NOTE    First Session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance  6<U+2013>7 July 2026 I Palexpo, Geneva    Digital Public Goods and Societal AI Resilience: Bridging Capacity Gaps in the  Global Majority      Date & Time:  6 July 2026, 18:30<U+2013>19:15 CEST    Organizers:  Policy Hub for Inclusive Development (PHID) <U+00B7> Olabi    Background and focus  Artificial intelligence (AI) is rapidly reshaping economies, public institutions, and societies  worldwide. While global AI governance discussions have made significant progress in  advancing principles related to safety, transparency, interoperability, and human rights,  implementation capacity remains highly uneven across countries. For many governments and  institutions in the Global Majority, the challenge is no longer simply accessing AI technologies,  but developing the institutional readiness required to govern, deploy, and benefit from AI  responsibly.  This session explores the role of Digital Public Goods (DPGs) as foundational infrastructure for  societal AI resilience. Open-source, interoperable, and reusable digital solutions can  strengthen public institutions, accelerate capacity-building, and reduce barriers to responsible  AI adoption. The discussion will examine how investments in shared digital infrastructure and  institutional capabilities can help countries move from AI access to AI readiness, while  equipping governments, communities, and younger generations to navigate an AI-driven  future.  Hosted by the Policy Hub for Inclusive Development (PHID) in collaboration with Olabi, the  session will take the form of an interactive fireside chat connecting practical experience with  global policy discussions. It will explore how governments, civil society, and international  organizations can leverage DPGs to strengthen public institutions, foster inclusive innovation,  and enhance international cooperation in support of effective, accountable, and inclusive  governance.  The discussion aims to contribute practical policy insights to the Global Dialogue on AI  Governance by highlighting scalable pathways for strengthening institutional capacity and   advancing more inclusive and resilient AI ecosystems across the Global Majority. Key  takeaways and recommendations from the discussion will be synthesized into a policy memo  to be shared with the Joint Secretariat and the Co-Chairs of the Global Dialogue on AI  Governance as a contribution from the session.  Speakers and Format  Format: Fireside Chat (45 minutes)  The discussion will be guided by the following questions:  <U+25CF><U+200B> How can DPGs help reduce inequalities in AI adoption across the Global Majority?  <U+25CF><U+200B> What are the main capacity and institutional challenges that countries in the Global  Majority face in preparing for the AI era?  <U+25CF><U+200B> Which experiences in majority countries demonstrate the potential of open digital  solutions to strengthen institutional capacity, foster inclusive innovation, and build  societal AI resilience?  <U+25CF><U+200B> How can digital inclusion and DPGs be more effectively integrated into the global AI  governance agenda?  Moderator  Michelle Perino, MPA (London School of Economics and Political Science), is Co-CEO of the  Policy Hub for Inclusive Development (PHID) and a specialist in poverty, inequality, and social  policy. Her work focuses on human rights, equality, and the design and implementation of  social policy programmes, advising governments, regulatory bodies, and private sector  organizations across Europe and Latin America. At PHID, she leads programme design and  delivery, connecting climate action, socioeconomic empowerment, and digital development to  advance inclusive and sustainable public policy solutions.  Speaker  Gabriela Agustini is Co-Executive Director and Co-Founder of Olabi, a Brazilian civil society  organization advancing digital inclusion, AI governance, and technological justice across the  Global South. An Ashoka Fellow, journalist, and researcher, she has spent more than a  decade designing initiatives that strengthen communities' agency in emerging technologies  through capacity building, participatory governance, open innovation, and public engagement.  Her work bridges technology, public policy, and social impact, with a particular focus on AI  governance, open-source ecosystems, and the inclusion of historically underrepresented  communities.  Focal Point/Contact:  Mariana Beselga  Co-CEO  Policy Hub for Inclusive Development (PHID)  mariana@phid.org

CONCEPT NOTE First Session of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance 6<U+2013>7 July 2026 I Palexpo, Geneva Human-Centred AI Governance: Bridging Skills, Ethics, Inclusion and the  Future of Work Date & Time: 7 July 2026, 13:30<U+2013>14:15 Geneva CEST | (45-minute online side event) Organiser: Academy Achievers (#BeMeDigitalInclusion) Artificial intelligence is reshaping every sector of society, making inclusive and human- centred governance more important than ever. This interactive keynote explores  practical approaches to AI governance through the lenses of skills development, ethics,  digital inclusion and the future of work, demonstrating how global principles can be  translated into meaningful action across education, industry and communities. Background and focus Artificial intelligence is transforming economies, education systems, labour markets and  public services at an unprecedented pace. While AI presents significant opportunities to  accelerate innovation and sustainable development, it also raises important questions  about governance, ethics, equity, workforce transformation and public trust. As  governments and organisations develop AI policies and regulatory frameworks, there is  an increasing need to ensure that implementation remains human-centred, inclusive and  responsive to the needs of diverse communities. This keynote explores how human-centred AI governance can bridge the gap between  global policy discussions and practical implementation. Drawing on experiences from  education, workforce development, digital inclusion and responsible AI initiatives across   the United Kingdom and Africa, the session will examine how governments, educators,  industry and civil society can work together to build AI ecosystems that create  opportunity while protecting human dignity and promoting trust. The keynote will focus on five interconnected themes: responsible AI governance; AI  literacy and skills development; ethics and public trust; digital inclusion; and the future of  work. Particular emphasis will be placed on ensuring women, young people and  underserved communities are not only prepared for an AI-enabled economy but are  actively involved in shaping it. Aligned with the objectives of the Global Dialogue on AI Governance, the session will  encourage international collaboration, knowledge exchange and practical capacity- building. Participants will gain actionable insights that support responsible AI adoption,  strengthen digital capability and contribute to inclusive, resilient and trusted AI  ecosystems. The discussion also supports broader efforts towards achieving the  Sustainable Development Goals by promoting equitable access to AI opportunities and  ensuring that technological progress benefits society as a whole. Speakers and Format Format: 45-minute interactive online keynote presentation followed by moderated  audience discussion. Speaker: Paulette Watson MBE <U+2013> Founder and Managing Director, Academy Achievers;  Global Lead, #BeMeDigitalInclusion. Paulette Watson MBE is an AI governance advisor, educator, author and international  speaker whose work focuses on responsible AI, digital inclusion, STEM education and  workforce transformation across the United Kingdom and Africa. Through Academy  Achievers and the #BeMeDigitalInclusion initiative, she collaborates with governments,  educational institutions, industry and community organisations to expand equitable  access to digital skills and support human-centred approaches to AI adoption. The keynote will explore practical strategies for translating AI governance principles into  action through education, workforce development and inclusive innovation. Drawing on  cross-sector experience and international collaboration, the presentation will  demonstrate how responsible AI can strengthen public trust, expand participation and  prepare communities for the future of work.  The final part of the session will be dedicated to audience questions and discussion,  encouraging participants to exchange perspectives, identify shared challenges and  explore practical actions that advance responsible, inclusive and human-centred AI  governance across regions and sectors. She has contributed to responsible AI and digital inclusion initiatives across the UK and  Africa and regularly advises organisations on human-centred AI, workforce  transformation and digital skills.
